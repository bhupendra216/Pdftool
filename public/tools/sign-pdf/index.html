Sign PDF

Add one or more drawn, typed, or uploaded signatures to a PDF, position them on the page, and save the signed document on your device.

How it works

  1. Upload a PDF and choose a page to preview.
  2. Draw, type, or upload a signature, then place and position it on the page.
  3. Apply your signatures and download the signed PDF.

Why use this tool

Three ways to sign

Draw with touch or a mouse, type your name in a handwriting-style font, or upload a signature image.

Position with control

Move and resize each signature, choose a color, and optionally add today's date.

Local processing

Your PDF and signature image are processed in your browser and never uploaded to our servers.

When to use Sign PDF

Add one or more drawn, typed, or uploaded signatures to a PDF, position them on the page, and save the signed document on your device. This workflow is useful when you need a quick result without installing a desktop PDF application. It creates a separate output file, so you can compare the result with the source before sharing it.

Routine document work

Use this tool for finished documents that need one focused operation before an upload, review, meeting, or handoff. Check the source first, choose the smallest useful page range, and give the downloaded file a clear name.

Private working copies

The page is designed for browser-based processing. Even when a workflow runs locally, you should still use a trusted device, keep your browser updated, and avoid leaving downloaded documents in a shared downloads folder.

Final quality check

PDF structures vary. Fonts, scans, tables, signatures, forms, and unusual page layouts can affect results, so open the output in a PDF viewer and verify the portions that matter before relying on it.

Technical details and limitations

Input
PDF files up to 50MB and 20 pages, subject to the limits shown by the upload control.
Output
A PDF or document output described above; the exact result depends on the selected workflow and source structure.
Processing
Browser-side processing is used for this tool. Large, image-heavy, encrypted, damaged, or complex files can require more memory or may not process successfully.
  • Review converted tables, recognized text, placed signatures, or other generated content manually.
  • Password-protected or corrupted PDFs may need to be repaired or unlocked before processing.
  • This tool is not a substitute for a certified signature provider, document-management system, or accessibility review.
  • Keep the original document until the downloaded result has been checked.

Practical tips

  • Use a clear, upright source PDF with readable text whenever possible.
  • Process fewer pages when a large file causes browser memory pressure.
  • Use the related tools below when the document needs another step before or after this workflow.
  • Do not upload a document unless you have permission to process and share it.

Frequently asked questions

  • How do I add a signature to a PDF?

    Draw with your mouse or finger, type your name in a signature font, or upload an image of your signature. Place and resize it anywhere on the document.

  • Is a PDFKira signature legally binding?

    A drawn signature marks intent, but legal validity depends on your jurisdiction and whether the document requires a certified digital signature (with certificate). For contracts requiring certification, use a dedicated e-signature service.

  • Does signing happen in my browser?

    Yes. The signature is applied locally and the file downloads straight from your browser — no upload required.

  • Can I add the date next to my signature?

    Yes — add a text field alongside your signature and type the date, or include it when typing your signature.
